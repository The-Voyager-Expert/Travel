Lounges

Every airport lounge we cover, in one list, country by country — where each one sits inside the terminal, when it opens, and precisely which ticket, alliance status, membership card or walk-in fee gets you through the door. Airline and alliance lounges, Priority Pass and its rivals, credit-card lounges and pay-at-the-door rooms are all here, along with the visit caps, guest rules and terminal splits that are the usual reason someone gets turned away.

No lounges match that search.
How to Access
Airline & alliance — your cabin on the day, or your status with that airline's alliance. A business-class ticket admits you to that alliance's lounges; oneworld Sapphire, Star Alliance Gold and SkyTeam Elite Plus admit you in economy. Membership cards never open these doors
Pay at the door — anyone can buy in, no ticket class and no card needed. Rates run from about $25 to $100 for two to three hours and are usually cheaper booked online than at the desk
Membership — Priority Pass is the widest network, with LoungeKey and DragonPass covering many of the same rooms; which of the three your card carries depends on the bank that issued it. Most lounges cap a membership visit at two or three hours, and busy lounges turn members away at the door before they turn away ticketed passengers
Credit card — a lounge network the card issuer owns outright, separate from Priority Pass. American Express Centurion is the largest; entry needs the card and a same-day flight, guests are charged, and annual visit caps apply on the lower card tiers
