4.Bañuelo
A Nasrid public bath from the 11th century, built along the Darro river at the base of the Alhambra hill. Five domed chambers — cold, tepid, and hot rooms — survive largely intact, with star-shaped skylights piercing brick vaults and horseshoe arches carried on Roman-era capitals reused from earlier buildings.

2.Palacio de la Madraza
Founded by Sultan Yusuf I in 1349, Granada's first madrasa served as a Quranic school before becoming the town hall after the Reconquista. The Baroque exterior conceals the original Nasrid oratory — its mihrab covered in 14th-century stucco tracery equal to anything inside the Alhambra.

3.Alcaicería
A web of covered lanes occupying the site of the original Nasrid silk souk, rebuilt in Romantic-Moorish style after the 1843 fire. Lanterns, carved stucco, and tilework line the narrow passages; the market's silk trade has given way to ceramics, leather goods, and spice vendors alongside the Cathedral.

4.Corral del Carbón
Built around 1336 as a Nasrid caravanserai for the city's silk merchants, the Corral del Carbón is the oldest Islamic monument in Granada. The horseshoe gateway with its carved plaster frieze and the central courtyard survive intact; after 1492 the building became a coal yard, then a theatre.

From Hotel: 🚶 25 min · 🚕 8 min → Carmen de los Mártires
1.Carmen de los Mártires
A 19th-century villa on the Alhambra's western slope, surrounded by terraced gardens built over the site of a Carmelite convent where Christian prisoners were held during the Nasrid period. The grounds include an English romantic section, a French formal garden, a small lake, and a reconstructed Nasrid patio courtyard.

🚕 7 min → Fundación Rodríguez-Acosta
2.Fundación Rodríguez-Acosta
A Carmen-studio built between 1914 and 1930 for Granadan painter José María Rodríguez-Acosta, descending the Alhambra's southern slope across four terraced gardens. The subterranean galleries below house the Instituto Gómez-Moreno — Roman mosaics, Islamic ivories, pre-Columbian ceramics, and medieval sculpture.

3.Huerta de San Vicente
The García Lorca family's summer home from 1926 to 1936, where Federico wrote Blood Wedding, Yerma, and the Divan del Tamarit. The house is preserved as a working interior — Lorca's piano, desk, and personal library remain in place — surrounded by a small garden now absorbed into Parque García Lorca.
