🏰 A Widow Built a Miniature Castle on This Island Just to Serve Tea

Fannette Island — the only island on the entire lake — carries a stone building on its summit that exists for one reason: afternoon tea. When Lora Knight built her Vikingsholm estate on the shore of Emerald Bay in the late 1920s, she had a construction crew of roughly 200 workers also raise a tiny stone tea house on the island's peak, finished within the same few months as the main house. The whole structure is barely large enough for a table, four chairs, and a small fireplace. A few times each summer, Knight would have staff motor her and her guests out to the island so they could sip tea in a one-room castle while looking back at the larger castle she'd just built on shore.

Marlette Lake wasn't preserved for its views — it was built to solve a crisis. When the Comstock Lode silver boom in Virginia City, Nevada, outgrew its water supply in the 1870s, engineers ran a pipeline down from this alpine lake and pushed the water across an entire valley using an inverted siphon under more pressure than any pipe on Earth had ever sustained at the time — no pumps, just gravity and sealed iron pipe forcing water uphill on the far side. Portions of that 19th-century engineering feat are still in use today, quietly supplying water to Virginia City more than a century after silver stopped being the reason anyone needed it.
