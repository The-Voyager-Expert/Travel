From Hotel: 🚕 21 min → The Grotto
1. The Grotto
A 62-acre Catholic outdoor sanctuary set at the base and top of a 110-foot basalt cliff in northeast Portland. The lower plaza is free and centers on a marble Pietà set into a natural cave; an elevator ascends to the upper level, where botanical gardens, a meditation chapel, and a terrace with views of Mounts St. Helens, Adams, and Rainier surround the cliff edge.
📖 Read more
🏛️ Daily 9:00am - 8:00pm (March - September)
⏰ ~2 hr
📍 NE 85th Avenue & Sandy Boulevard · Cully

2. Portland Aerial Tram
Portland's two-car aerial tram connects the South Waterfront to OHSU's Marquam Hill campus 500 feet above in a 3-minute ride. The upper station opens onto a public terrace with panoramic views over the Willamette River, downtown Portland, and the volcanic peaks of Mount Hood, Mount St. Helens, and Mount Adams.

🍮 Local Tastes
Marionberry Pie
Oregon's signature blackberry cultivar, grown almost exclusively in the Willamette Valley. Shows up in pies, jam, ice cream, and pastries across the city.
📍 Ken's Artisan Bakery · 338 NW 21st Avenue
Voodoo Doughnut
Portland's cult doughnut institution on SW Third Avenue. Known for outlandish toppings, the bacon maple bar, and the Voodoo Doll — a raspberry-filled doughnut served with a pretzel stake.
📍 Voodoo Doughnut Old Town · 22 SW 3rd Avenue
Dungeness Crab
The Pacific Northwest's premier shellfish, fished from waters off the Oregon coast. In season November through June, served cracked whole at restaurants sourcing from Pacific Coast boats.
📍 Jake's Famous Crawfish · 401 SW 12th Avenue
Stumptown Coffee
Portland's original third-wave coffee roaster, founded on SE Division. The Hair Bender espresso blend — named for the salon that preceded the café — is a city staple at the original location and across the city.
📍 Stumptown Coffee Division · 4525 SE Division Street

🌲 Portland's Secret Geometry

Portland sits at the confluence of two major rivers — the Willamette and the Columbia — and three mountain views: Hood, St. Helens, and Adams. Almost every high point in the city, from Pittock Mansion to Mount Tabor's summit, frames at least one of these volcanoes on a clear day. The city's grid was deliberately designed with narrow 200-foot blocks — nearly half the standard US block size — which gives downtown Portland more street corners per mile than almost any American city. That's why walking feels so natural here.

The International Rose Test Garden was established in 1917 partly as a safe haven — rose cultivars were shipped from European gardens threatened by World War I, making Portland's hillside a kind of living ark for endangered varieties. Over a century later, the garden still receives trial varieties that may never be sold commercially.
