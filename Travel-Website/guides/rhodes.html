Day 4
From Hotel: 🚕 25 min → Anthony Quinn Bay
1.Anthony Quinn Bay
Sheltered turquoise cove where Anthony Quinn bought land while filming The Guns of Navarone on Rhodes in 1961; the Greek government reclaimed it in 1984 under property law but the name endured; two rocky inlets frame exceptionally clear water with good snorkelling along the submerged rock walls.
🏛️ Open 24/7
⏰ ~1 hr
📍 Ladiko Bay · Faliraki

2.Acropolis of Lindos
Ancient hilltop sanctuary 116 metres above Lindos Bay, where the Doric Temple of Athena Lindia — built around 300 BC — crowns the summit; the site layers Hellenistic colonnades, a medieval Knights Hospitaller fortress, and a Byzantine chapel on a single dramatic rock overlooking the horseshoe harbour and open Aegean.

Day 5
From Hotel: 🚕 55 min → Embonas
1.Embonas
Mountain village at 550 metres on the slopes of Mount Attavyros, the highest peak on Rhodes; the island's wine capital grows Athiri and Mandilaria grapes at CAIR cooperative and Emery wineries, and the village square is lined with tavernas serving oven dishes poured from ceramic mugs of local bulk wine.
📖 Read more
🏛️ Open 24/7
⏰ ~1 hr
📍 Embonas · Mount Attavyros
🚕 30 min → Monolithos Castle
2.Monolithos Castle
Knights Hospitaller fortress from 1476 built atop a solitary 100-metre limestone monolith rising from the western coastline; a rock staircase leads to walls that were never conquered, enclosing a still-functioning chapel of Agios Panteleimon with panoramic views across the channel to Chalki island.
📖 Read more
🏛️ Open 24/7
⏰ ~1 hr
📍 Monolithos · West Rhodes
🚕 35 min → Prasonisi
3.Prasonisi
The southernmost point of Rhodes where the Aegean and Mediterranean meet on opposite sides of a sandy tombolo connecting the rocky cape to the mainland in summer; the Aegean side delivers consistent waves for experienced windsurfers and kitesurfers while the Mediterranean side stays flat and clear for swimming.

🏛️ Open 24/7

🍮 Local Tastes
Pitaroudia
Crispy chickpea and herb fritters unique to Rhodes — a street-food staple sold at local bakeries and markets across the island since ancient times.
📍 Fournariko · Central Market
Melekouni
Traditional Rhodian candy made from sesame seeds, honey, and orange zest — exchanged at weddings and sold year-round in confectionery shops across the island.
📍 Old Town sweet shops
Souma
Rhodian mulberry spirit distilled in the villages of the interior — fiery, aromatic, and traditionally drunk straight or used in cooking.
📍 Village kafeneions · Rhodes interior
Soumada
Chilled almond-milk syrup drink served on Rhodes as a non-alcoholic refresher — sweet, milky, and distinctly Rhodian. Offered at traditional cafes in the Old Town.
📍 Mevlana Cafe · Socratous Garden
Rhodian Thyme Honey
Raw monofloral honey produced by bees foraging on wild thyme that blankets Rhodes's hillsides — intensely aromatic and sold in jars across the island.

Arrive at the Palace of the Grand Masters at 8:00am when it opens on a non-Monday — you'll have the courtyard entirely to yourself before the first tour buses arrive around 10:00am.

Rent a car for Day 3. Public KTEL buses connect Rhodes Town to individual sites but not to Filerimos, Valley of the Butterflies, Kameiros, and Kritinia Castle as a connected loop in a single day.

The Valley of the Butterflies is only impressive from late June through August, when Jersey tiger moths cluster in the thousands. Visit outside that window and you'll see an ordinary wooded valley.

Book Thermes Kallithea online before arriving — the restored Art Deco complex is compact and can feel crowded by mid-morning in peak summer. An early 8:00am arrival gives you the architecture in golden light before swimmers arrive.

In the Medieval Town, most shops and restaurants close for lunch from roughly 2:00pm to 6:00pm in summer. Plan the Sokratous Street stroll for the cool of the evening rather than the afternoon.
