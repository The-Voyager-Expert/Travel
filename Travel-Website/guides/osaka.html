From Hotel: 🚶 33 min · 🚕 12 min → Osaka Tenmangu
1. Osaka Tenmangu
A Shinto shrine founded in 949 dedicated to Sugawara no Michizane, patron of scholarship. The annual Tenjin Matsuri held here each July ranks among Japan's three greatest festivals. The main hall's ornate roof carvings repay close inspection.

The city's defining landmark, a reconstructed castle tower rising above vast moats and stone walls. Eight floors of museum exhibits chart Toyotomi Hideyoshi's unification campaigns. The observation deck commands a full-circle panorama over the central skyline.

4. Isshinji
An 1185 temple adjacent to Shitennoji, known for the Okotsu Butsudan — a series of Buddha statues cast from the compressed ash and bones of cremated donors, made every 10 years since 1887. Each statue incorporates the remains of approximately 200,000 people. The main hall is a striking 1957 concrete building.

Day 2
From Hotel: 🚕 17 min → Tempozan Ferris Wheel
1. Tempozan Ferris Wheel
A 112.5-meter Ferris wheel at Tempozan Harbor Village on Osaka Bay. When it opened in 1997 it was the world's largest; it still ranks among the tallest in Japan. Each gondola circuit takes 15 minutes; transparent-floor gondolas are available on request. The nightly illumination forecasts next-day weather by color.

2. Osaka Aquarium Kaiyukan
One of the world's largest aquariums, built around a central Pacific Ocean tank nine metres deep housing whale sharks. A descending spiral ramp passes through 15 habitat zones from Arctic ice to tropical reef. Over 30,000 creatures across 620 species.

1. Nakanoshima Library
A 1904 Greco-Roman building on Nakanoshima island, one of Japan's three national historic libraries. Designated an Important Cultural Property. The symmetric facade with paired columns and central dome was designed by Noda Tokutaro. The reading room retains original carved wood shelves.

2. Osaka Central Public Hall
A 1918 Baroque-Renaissance civic building on Nakanoshima island, designated an Important Cultural Property. The domed central tower and red-brick exterior were designed after European civic halls; the interior has ornate assembly rooms used for concerts and exhibitions. A café-restaurant operates in the basement.

A science museum with four floors of hands-on exhibits on electricity, space, life, and chemistry — including a walk-through human heart model and a Mars surface simulator. The rooftop planetarium shows programs on a 4K dome. Located on the western tip of Nakanoshima island.

4. National Museum of Art Osaka
An entirely underground museum housing a 3,500-work collection of post-1945 Japanese and international art. The only above-ground element is Cesar Pelli's twin steel arch — a 43-meter sculpture that marks the entrance. The museum is one of Japan's five national museums and admission is low.

2. Shitenno-ji
Japan's oldest officially established Buddhist temple, founded in 593 CE by Prince Shotoku. The classic Indian-style arrangement of gates, pagoda, and main hall follows a layout unchanged in form for 14 centuries. The outer precinct and the stone torii gate at the west entrance are free to enter.

3. Osaka Museum of Natural History
The largest natural history museum in western Japan, in Nagai Park. The collection spans Osaka's geology, local ecosystems, and a full dinosaur gallery with mounted skeletons. The permanent collection is free; a lotus pond at the entrance blooms in summer. Park walking paths wrap the building.

4. Sumiyoshi Taisha
One of Japan's oldest shrines, founded in the third century before Buddhism arrived. Its four main halls use the pure Sumiyoshi-zukuri style with straight roofs free of continental influence. The steeply arched Sorihashi Bridge is the visual centrepiece.

1. Minō Falls
A 33-meter waterfall in Meiji no Mori Minō Quasi-National Park, reached by a 2.5 km trail from Minoo Station. The path follows a river through cedar forest, passing tea houses selling tempura maple leaves — a local specialty. The surrounding maples blaze red in autumn.

2. Katsuo-ji
A 7th-century temple on Mount Minoo covered in thousands of red daruma dolls placed by worshippers praying for victory in sports, exams, and business. The main hall is a Muromachi-period Important Cultural Property. Mossy stone steps and forested mountain setting make it one of the region's most striking temples.

🍮 Local Tastes
Takoyaki
Crisp-shelled wheat batter balls filled with diced octopus, pickled ginger, and green onion. Invented in 1935. Finished with thick sauce, mayonnaise, bonito flakes, and seaweed.
📍 Wanaka Sennichimae · Takoyaki Juhachiban · Aizuya
Okonomiyaki
A savoury cabbage pancake griddled with pork, egg, and a yam-enriched batter. The local style mixes all ingredients before cooking, unlike Hiroshima's layered method. Each table has its own teppan griddle.
📍 Fukutaro · Mizuno · Ajinoya
Kushikatsu
Deep-fried skewers of meat, seafood, and vegetables coated in panko and dipped once in a communal sauce. Double-dipping is famously forbidden. Shinsekai is the original heartland.
📍 Daruma Dotonbori · Yaekatsu · Kushikatsu Tanaka
Kitsune Udon
Thick wheat noodles in light dashi broth topped with a large slab of sweetened fried tofu. Created in the 1890s at Usami Tei Matsubaya, the city's oldest surviving udon shop.
📍 Usami Tei Matsubaya · Tsurutontan Dotonbori

The city's identity is built on street food in a way no other Japanese city matches. Dotonbori and Shinsekai offer the purest version of kuidaore — eating yourself bankrupt — the local philosophy of prioritizing food over appearances. The combination of accessible shrines, castle history, modern observation points, and a food culture dense enough to fill three days makes this one of the most rewarding short trips in East Asia.
