3. Sutter's Fort
Adobe fort built in 1839 by John Sutter, the first non-Native American settlement in Central Valley. Gold was discovered at Sutter's Mill in 1848, triggering the Gold Rush; the fort served as supply point and hospital for overland emigrants on the California Trail. Restored to 1846 with a self-guided audio tour.

Worth Knowing

Two Endings

Sutro Baths had already been sold to developers with plans for a high-rise, and demolition crews were partway through tearing the building down when a fire broke out on June 26, 1966 and finished the job in a single afternoon — faster than the wrecking crew had managed in months. Investigators suspected arson from the start; insurance fraud and stray fireworks were both floated as explanations, but no one was ever charged and the exact cause was never settled. What's left is the concrete ruin visitors walk through today, burned into its current shape rather than demolished into it.

The bronze triptych behind the altar in Grace Cathedral's AIDS memorial chapel was one of the last things Keith Haring ever made. He completed The Life of Christ only weeks before he died of AIDS-related complications in 1990 — one of the very few religious works in a career built almost entirely on street-art figures and radiant outlines. It wasn't installed and unveiled until 1995, on World AIDS Day, five years after his death.
