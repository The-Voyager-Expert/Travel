From Hotel: 🚕 10 min → Yamato Museum
1.Yamato Museum
The Kure City Maritime History and Science Museum — a 26.3-metre 1/10-scale model of battleship Yamato, built from the original construction blueprints and underwater survey footage, dominates the main hall. Yamato was the heaviest and most powerfully armed battleship ever commissioned; also on display: a real Type 62 Zero fighter and a Kaiten human torpedo.

2.JMSDF Kure Museum
The only museum in Japan where visitors board a real decommissioned submarine — the JS Akishio, a Yūshio-class vessel that served from 1986 to 2004, stands alongside the building. Walk through the control room, crew quarters, and captain's office; the operational periscopes provide views of Hiroshima Bay.

From Hotel: 🚕 10 min → Ōkunoshima Poison Gas Museum
1.Ōkunoshima Poison Gas Museum
A small museum documenting the secret Imperial Army chemical weapons factory that operated on this island from 1929 to 1945 — mustard gas, lewisite, and tear gas were produced here and deployed in the invasion of China. Exhibits include original production equipment, workers' diaries, and photographs documenting the factory operations.

2.Ōkunoshima Ruins
The island's 4-km coastal path passes the crumbling concrete shells of the Imperial Army's poison gas production buildings — storage bunkers, ventilation towers, and factory foundations now reclaimed by vegetation. Hundreds of wild rabbits inhabit the island and approach visitors freely along the ruins path.

🍮 Local Tastes
Okonomiyaki · Hiroshima-style
Unlike the Osaka version where ingredients are mixed into batter, Hiroshima okonomiyaki is built in distinct layers: batter, cabbage, pork, and soba noodles cooked on an iron griddle. The style grew from postwar scarcity.
📍 Okonomimura · Nagataya
Momiji Manju · maple leaf cakes
A baked wagashi shaped like a Japanese maple leaf — red bean paste filling is standard, with matcha, custard, and chocolate variants. The shape is inspired by the maples on Mt. Misen. Omotesando on Miyajima has the highest concentration.
📍 Omotesando Street · Miyajima Island
Oysters · Seto Inland Sea
Hiroshima Prefecture produces about 60% of Japan's oysters from the sheltered Seto Inland Sea. Available year-round; best October through March. On Miyajima, street stalls along Omotesando grill them fresh on the shell.
📍 Miyajima oyster stalls
Anago Meshi · conger eel on rice
Miyajima's signature dish — conger eel glazed with sweet soy tare, served over rice in a wooden box. Distinct from Tokyo's unagi: leaner texture. Ueno on Miyajima's Omotesando is the primary specialist.
📍 Ueno · Miyajima Island

❗️ Heads Up
Hiroshima Castle — Keep Closed for Renovation
The main tower is closed for seismic renovation; grounds and Sannomaru cultural facility remain fully open.
Peace Memorial Museum — Book Tickets in Advance
Walk-up queues can exceed one hour during peak periods — book in advance via the museum's official website.
Otorii Gate — Tide-Dependent Beach Access
Walking to the gate's base is only possible at low tide — check the tide schedule before visiting.
Day 3 Kure — Both Museums Closed Tuesday
Both the Yamato Museum and JMSDF Kure Museum are closed on Tuesday. Schedule Day 3 on any other day of the week.
Day 4 Ōkunoshima — Last Ferry Timing
Sanyo Shosen ferries run every 30–45 min between Tadanoumi Port and Ōkunoshima. Last return ferry around 7:15pm in summer; earlier in winter.

🕊️ The Deliberate Admission

The Peace Memorial Museum has kept its admission at one of the lowest of any major international museum since it opened in 1955. The board's reasoning, restated by successive directors, is that the weight of what the museum documents should not be a financial barrier for anyone from anywhere. It is an unusual institutional philosophy for a museum of this historical gravity.

The hotel sits on Heiwa Odori — Peace Boulevard — a 100-metre-wide road running 4 kilometres through central Hiroshima. The boulevard is far wider than traffic demands. It was designed that way in the 1950s postwar reconstruction plan as a deliberate firebreak: if another disaster struck the city, the road's width would stop the fire from crossing. The unusual dimensions are not a planning accident. They are built into the street as a permanent, functional reminder of what happened on August 6, 1945.
