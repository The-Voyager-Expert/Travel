1. Stirling Castle
Perched on a volcanic crag above the Forth valley, Stirling Castle was the preferred seat of the Stuart monarchs — the Renaissance Great Hall, the Royal Palace interiors restored to their 1540s splendour and views from Ben Lomond to the Ochil Hills make this Scotland's most dramatic royal residence.
📖 Wikipedia

2. Church of the Holy Rude
One of the oldest still-used churches in Scotland — James VI was crowned here in 1567 with John Knox preaching the sermon, and the medieval nave dating from the 1400s survives largely intact. The churchyard above Stirling commands views across the Forth valley.
📖 Wikipedia
🏛️ Daily 10:00am - 6:00pm
⏰ ~30 min
⚠️ Open 25 March - 1 November only.
📍 Saint John's Street · Stirling Old Town
🚶 28 min · 🚕 7 min → Wallace Monument
3. Wallace Monument
A 220-foot Victorian Gothic tower rising from Abbey Craig above the site of Wallace's 1297 victory at Stirling Bridge — the Wallace Sword, a life-cast of his head and the Hall of Heroes are inside, with a panoramic view of the Forth valley, Stirling and the Highland edge from the crown.

✨ A City That Makes Things

Glasgow rewards walking with your head tilted up. The wealth of the shipbuilding and tobacco eras went straight into stone — red and blonde sandstone tenements, the muscular City Chambers, the spires of the old town — so the city centre reads like an open-air gallery of Victorian ambition. Then it keeps surprising you: Zaha Hadid's silver wave on the Clyde, the Kibble Palace's iron bubble of ferns, a medieval cathedral that quietly outlasted the Reformation.

It is also a city that makes things and feeds you well while doing it — a Cantonese institution older than most of its rivals, a Michelin kitchen above a tenement, a teacake empire in the suburbs. Give the West End an unhurried afternoon, take the little orange Subway at least once for the fun of it, and save an evening for a whisky bar where the list runs to four figures.
