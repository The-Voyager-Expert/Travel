Day 1
From Hotel: 🚕 2h 10min → Papakolea Green Sand Beach
1. Papakolea Green Sand Beach
One of very few green sand beaches in the world — its color comes from olivine crystals eroded from the walls of a cinder cone above the bay. The beach sits in a half-sheltered cove below ochre sea cliffs. Reached by a 5-mile round-trip walk from the Ka Lae parking area, or by informal jeep shuttles that operate from the lot.

🏛️ Open 24/7

📍 Papakolea Beach · Naʻālehu
🚕 15 min → Punaluʻu Black Sand Beach
2. Punaluʻu Black Sand Beach
One of the most striking beaches on earth — jet-black volcanic sand formed when lava shatters on contact with the ocean. Hawaiian green sea turtles haul out to bask on the warm black sand throughout the day. You can stand three feet away from an endangered species sunbathing on a beach that shouldn't exist.

🏛️ Open 24/7
⏰ ~45 min
📍 Ninole Loop Road · Pahala
🚕 1h → Manta Ray Night Snorkel
3. Manta Ray Night Snorkel
The Kona coast is one of the only places on Earth where giant Pacific manta rays gather every night without fail. Snorkelers float on the surface holding lit boards; the rays — up to 14-foot wingspans — spiral up through the light column inches below your face. One of the great wildlife encounters on the planet.

Day 2
From Hotel: 🚕 30 min → Kīholo Bay
1. Kīholo Bay
A remote bay reached by a short walk across ʻaʻā lava from the highway. The brackish Wainanalii Pond — fed by underground freshwater springs mixing with seawater — draws green sea turtles that rest in the warm shallows. The 1859 Mauna Loa lava flow encircled the bay, creating a landscape of black lava meeting turquoise water.

🚕 1h 15min → Puʻuhonua o Hōnaunau National Historical Park
2. Puʻuhonua o Hōnaunau National Historical Park
A sacred Hawaiian place of refuge where warriors defeated in battle and kapu violators could reach this sanctuary and be absolved. The Great Wall — ten feet high, seventeen feet thick — still stands. Reconstructed thatched houses and carved god figures face the sea. The most powerful historical site in Hawaii.

🚕 1h 15min → Mauna Kea
3. Mauna Kea
The summit of Mauna Kea rises above 40% of the Earth's atmosphere — 13 international observatories operate here. The free Visitor Information Station at 9,200 feet stays open until 9:00pm nightly for a laser sky tour. The Milky Way from here is genuinely overwhelming.

1. Hawaiʻi Volcanoes National Park
Home to two of the world's most active volcanoes. Walk the rim of Halemaʻumaʻu Crater, descend into the Thurston Lava Tube, and drive the Chain of Craters Road to where lava flows swallowed entire highways and met the sea. No other national park puts you this close to a live volcano.

A waterfall in Hilo within the Wailuku River State Recreation Area. The falls drop 80 feet over a natural lava cave. The viewing platform is directly above the cave opening, with the full drop visible from a single vantage point.

An overlook at the rim of Waipiʻo Valley on the Hamakua Coast. The valley is approximately 1 mile wide and 6 miles deep, carved by the Wailoa River. The valley floor is accessible only by 4WD vehicle or guided tour — the lookout at the top provides the full panorama.

2. Ka Lae
The southernmost point in the United States, where the Pacific extends unbroken to Antarctica. Ancient Hawaiians established one of their earliest settlements here — canoe mooring holes are still carved into the lava rocks at the water's edge. The cliffs drop sheer into deep-blue water that turns jade green in the shallows. The trade winds are constant and relentless.

Day 5
From Hotel: 🚕 20 min → Puako Petroglyph Preserve
1. Puako Petroglyph Preserve
One of the most extensive accessible petroglyph fields in Hawaii, with approximately 3,000 images carved into smooth pāhoehoe lava. Human figures, canoes, and religious symbols cover the rock surface — the oldest predate 1500. A 1.4-mile round-trip boardwalk trail leads through dry lava to the field.

2. Lapakahi State Historical Park
The ruins of a 600-year-old Hawaiian fishing village on the North Kohala coast, among the best-preserved ancient coastal settlements on the island. Stone foundations, canoe sheds, salt pans, and an ancient konane game board have been partially restored along the shoreline. A self-guided trail with numbered markers runs 1 mile through the site.

🚕 20 min → Moʻokini Heiau
3. Moʻokini Heiau
One of the oldest and most sacred heiau in Hawaii, established in the 5th century CE according to oral tradition and rebuilt in the 13th century. The massive enclosure walls stand up to 30 feet tall. The birthplace of Kamehameha the Great — marked by a low stone monument — is a short walk from the entrance. The site is remote and almost always uncrowded.

Day 6
From Hotel: 🚕 15 min → Ahu'ena Heiau
1. Ahu'ena Heiau
King Kamehameha I rebuilt this heiau in 1813 on a small island in Kailua Bay, dedicating it to the god Lono. Three structures stood here — Hale Mana, Hale Pahu, and the ʻAnuʻu oracle tower — the tallest figure incorporates a golden plover, associated with early Polynesian navigation. Kamehameha died nearby in 1819. The platform is restricted but viewable from Kailua Pier.

⏰ ~45 min
Free
📍 Kailua Pier · Kailua-Kona
🚶 5 min · 🚕 5 min → Hulihe'e Palace
2. Hulihe'e Palace
Built in 1838 by Governor John Adams Kuakini, the palace served as a vacation retreat for Hawaiian royalty, most notably King Kalākaua and Queen Kapiʻolani. The Daughters of Hawaiʻi have operated it as a museum since 1927, displaying Victorian furnishings, royal artifacts, and Hawaiian koa wood pieces. The building sits on Ali'i Drive on the Kailua-Kona waterfront.

🚫 Closed Sunday - Tuesday

🚕 20 min → Holualoa Village
3. Holualoa Village
A small upland community in the Kona coffee belt where working coffee farms and art galleries share the same two-lane main road. The art scene has occupied former storefronts along Mamalahoa Highway since the 1960s; farms including UCC Hawaii offer walk-in tours during morning hours. Cloud cover rolls in by early afternoon — mornings give clearer views.

🍮 Local Tastes
Poke
The original Hawaiian preparation: raw ahi tuna with sea salt, roasted kukui nut, seaweed, and soy. Kona's poke comes from fish caught that morning — a completely different product from mainland versions.
📍 Umeke's Fish Market · Da Poke Shack
Kona Coffee
Grown on Mauna Loa's volcanic slopes between 800 and 2,500 feet, Kona coffee produces a delicate, low-acidity cup prized worldwide. Hand-harvested from small farms along Hwy 11.
📍 Greenwell Farms · Kona Coffee Living History Farm
Plate Lunch
The quintessential Hawaiian working lunch: two scoops of white rice, mac salad, and kalua pork, teriyaki beef, or chicken katsu. Born on the plantation when Japanese, Filipino, and Korean laborers shared food.
📍 75-5702 Kuakini Highway · 74-5617 Pawai Place
Loco Moco
Invented in Hilo: a bowl of white rice topped with a hamburger patty, a fried egg, and brown gravy. Pure comfort food that became a statewide institution. The Big Island is its birthplace.
📍 Cafe 100 · Hilo · 75-5702 Kuakini Highway

💡 On the Big Island

The Big Island is the only place on Earth where you can stand on an actively erupting volcano in the morning, swim beside an endangered species on a black sand beach in the afternoon, and lock eyes with a 14-foot manta ray spiraling through lit water at night.

Day 2 makes the case even more directly: a 3,500-year-old sanctuary still standing at the sea's edge, the finest reef snorkel in Hawaii in a bay where history ended for a famous explorer, and then the clearest sky on the planet two miles above sea level after dark. Most islands give you one or two of these. The Big Island gives you all of them in 48 hours.
