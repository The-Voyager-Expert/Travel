BEST LUXURIOUS HOTELS

The world's most luxurious hotels, and what separates them from the merely expensive
Hotels defined by service, design and setting rather than nightly rate alone. What separates them from the merely expensive is almost always something that cannot be built or scaled — a location that is no longer permitted, a building that cannot be replicated, or a staff ratio that a larger property could not sustain.
Argentina
Alvear Palace Hotel
Recoleta, Buenos Aires, Argentina
Since 1932Recoleta District
One of South America's pre-eminent grand hotels, a seven-storey Art Nouveau building on Avenida Alvear in the Recoleta district — the same boulevard as the French and Brazilian embassies. 189 rooms and suites furnished with period antiques and crystal chandeliers, with an original 1930s tearoom, L'Orangerie, that has served as the formal gathering place for Buenos Aires society for nine decades. The hotel operates independently, without any international chain affiliation.

Australia
Park Hyatt Sydney
Sydney, Australia
From USD 900/nightOpera House Views
155 rooms and suites on the edge of Sydney Cove, built so low to the ground that every harbour-facing room sits at eye level with the Opera House shells — not above them, not below, directly across the water. The rooftop pool mirrors the same view. One of the few hotels in the world where the landmark outside the window genuinely improves with proximity rather than distance.

Austria
Hotel Sacher Wien
Vienna, Austria
From USD 500/nightSince 1876
Directly opposite the Vienna State Opera and in the same family since Franz Sacher's son Eduard opened it in 1876, with 152 rooms furnished in deep reds and dark wood. The Sachertorte was invented here and is still made exclusively for the hotel under a legally protected recipe. The Rotes Bar is one of the most beautifully preserved red-lacquer rooms in any European hotel.

Bhutan
Amankora
Across Bhutan
From USD 1,800/nightFive Lodges, One Kingdom
Five private lodges across the Paro, Thimphu, Punakha, Gangtey, and Bumthang valleys — each capped at 24 guests. The standard journey moves east across the country over seven to ten days, with guided access to Tiger's Nest, Punakha Dzong, and restricted monasteries that require government permits Aman handles entirely. Bhutan's high daily fee effectively makes this the most regulated luxury travel experience in the world.

Siem Reap, Cambodia

A colonial-era landmark opened in 1929, built around a courtyard and pool that attracted Jacqueline Kennedy and Charlie Chaplin in its early decades. 119 rooms with French-colonial detailing, less than a kilometre from the gates of the Angkor temple complex — the closest full-service luxury hotel to the site. Canada
Fairmont Le Château Frontenac
Old Quebec, Quebec City, Canada
Since 1893World's Most Photographed Hotel
A château-style hotel built in 1893 on the Cap Diamant promontory above the St. Lawrence River, its copper-green turrets visible from kilometres away. Recognised by Guinness World Records as the world's most photographed hotel. 611 rooms inside a UNESCO World Heritage zone — the only fortified city north of the Rio Grande — with the Plains of Abraham on one side and Old Quebec's stone streets on the other. Winston Churchill and Franklin D. Roosevelt held a wartime strategy conference here in 1943.

Fifty-one suites in a restored Qing-dynasty complex within the grounds of the Summer Palace — a UNESCO World Heritage Site — accessible through a private imperial water gate sealed since the palace was completed in 1750. Denmark
Hotel d'Angleterre
Kongens Nytorv, Copenhagen, Denmark
Since 1755Europe's Oldest Grand Hotel
Opened in 1755 on Kongens Nytorv — the royal square linking the old city to Nyhavn — making it one of Europe's oldest continuously operating luxury hotels. 90 rooms after extensive renovation, with a spa, indoor pool, and terrace overlooking the square. Hans Christian Andersen and Winston Churchill are among the documented historical guests. The hotel was closed by the Gestapo during World War II and used as headquarters; it was returned to the original family after liberation and is still independently owned.

121 rooms on Carlos Place, Mayfair, operating since 1815 — originally as the Prince of Saxe-Coburg Hotel, renamed to The Connaught in 1917 to shed the Germanic name during World War I. Rosewood London
Holborn, London, United Kingdom
From USD 800/nightEdwardian Belle Époque
308 rooms in the 1914 Pearl Assurance Building, a Grade II-listed Edwardian Belle Époque facade that wraps around a cobblestoned courtyard off High Holborn. Scarfes Bar — named for the caricaturist Gerald Scarfe, whose original drawings line the walls — is consistently ranked among London's best hotel bars. The Grand Manor House wing has its own private entrance, kitchen, and rooftop terrace.

The Savoy
Strand, London, United Kingdom
From USD 650/nightSince 1889
267 rooms on the Strand since 1889, built by Richard D'Oyly Carte with profits from Gilbert and Sullivan operas performed at the adjacent Savoy Theatre. The hotel's forecourt is the only road in London where traffic drives on the right — engineered so cab passengers could step out curbside at the entrance. Kaspar, a two-foot-tall carved black cat, sits at any table of 13 as a permanent 14th guest — a tradition since 1927.

The Beverly Hills Hotel
Beverly Hills, California, United States
Since 1912The Pink Palace
A pink Mission Revival building on Sunset Boulevard, open since 1912 and nicknamed The Pink Palace for its signature flamingo-pink facade and tropical garden bungalows. The Polo Lounge, where Howard Hughes took the same booth every day for two decades, has been a deal-making institution for the Hollywood industry for a century. 23 bungalows in the grounds have hosted Marilyn Monroe (in Bungalow 1 during her divorce from Joe DiMaggio), Elizabeth Taylor (on multiple honeymoons), and virtually every major Hollywood star since the studio era. Owned by the Dorchester Collection and a National Register of Historic Places landmark.

The Plaza
New York, United States
From USD 900/nightNational Historic Landmark
282 rooms at the corner of Fifth Avenue and Central Park South since 1907, designated a National Historic Landmark in 1986. The Palm Court serves afternoon tea under a stained-glass ceiling, and the Oak Bar's dark-paneled interior has been a power-lunch institution for over a century. The fictional Eloise lived here in Kay Thompson's children's books — a painted portrait of her still hangs in the lobby. 24-karat gold fixtures throughout.

The St. Regis New York
New York, United States
From USD 700/nightBirthplace of the Bloody Mary
238 rooms on East 55th and Fifth Avenue since 1904, built by John Jacob Astor IV — who died on the Titanic eight years later. The King Cole Bar is the birthplace of the Bloody Mary (originally called the Red Snapper when it arrived from Paris in 1934), and the Maxfield Parrish mural behind the bar has hung in the same spot since opening day. Butler service is standard in every room — the St. Regis invented the concept of hotel butlers.

Sofitel Legend Metropole Hanoi
French Quarter, Hanoi, Vietnam
Since 1901Historic Wing Dating to French Indochina
A white neoclassical building in the French Quarter, open since 1901 and among the oldest continually operating luxury hotels in Southeast Asia. 364 rooms split between the historic wing and a 1990s extension. Charlie Chaplin honeymooned here in 1936; Graham Greene wrote part of The Quiet American in a corner suite during the Franco-Viet Minh War. Workers discovered a bomb shelter beneath the pool in 2012, used by guests including Joan Baez during the 1972 Christmas bombings — now accessible as a museum below the courtyard garden.
