GUIDE MY DAYS

Free travel guides, planned day by day — and the practical answers around them
Every guide is built the same way: what to see and in what order, how long each stop takes, and how to get between them on foot or by ride. Around the guides sit the things you look up once and need again — visas, plugs, trains, lounges, weather windows, safety.

There is more than one way in. Below, one section per tool — what it's for, what it looks at, and the filter itself. Start wherever your question actually is.

No destination in mind yet
Help me choose is the place to start if the answer is "somewhere warm in March, about a week, not too expensive." It asks four questions — when you're travelling, how long for, what weather you want, and what kind of trip it is — then returns only the cities that satisfy all four. It reads the real month-by-month climate data behind each guide, so "warm in March" means warm in March, not warm on average.

Start from the thing you want to see, not the city
Most travel sites are organised by destination: pick a city, read what's in it. That's backwards for a lot of trips. Nobody decides to go to Slovenia and then discovers caves — they want to see a cave, and the question is which one is worth the flight.
Best Of ranks a single category across every destination on the site. Not a top-ten listicle per country, but one comparable list: every castle worth routing a day around, ranked against each other, each with the city it sits in and a link straight into that city's guide. Thirty-four categories, from the obvious ones to the ones nobody else bothers to rank.

Your dates are already fixed
Most trips are decided by the fortnight you can take off, not by the destination. Work the other way round: name the weather you want and let the dates pick the place. Browse by Climate matches month against destination; When to Go gives the best window per city and the months worth avoiding.

You have a fixed number of days
Length decides which cities are worth it at all. Three days suits one dense city and not a country; ten opens up loops and day trips. Guides are written to a length rather than padded to fill one.
