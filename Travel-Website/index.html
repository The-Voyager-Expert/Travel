The part you won't find elsewhere

Start from the thing, not the city.

Most travel sites are organised by destination: pick a city, read what is in it. That is backwards for a lot of trips. Nobody decides to visit Slovenia and then discovers caves — they want to see a cave, and the question is which one is worth the flight.

Best Of ranks one category across every destination. Not a top-ten per country, but a single comparable list, each entry with the city it sits in and a link into that city's guide.

You know how many days you've got.

Length decides which cities are worth it at all. Three days suits one dense city and not a country; a week opens up loops and day trips; ten days is a different trip entirely.

Every guide here is written to a length rather than padded to fill one — a 3-day Lisbon is a genuinely different plan from a 6-day Lisbon, not the same plan stretched. Pick your number and you only see guides built for it.

Weather & climate

Most trips are decided by a fortnight, not a place.

You rarely get to pick both. The dates come from work, school or whoever you're travelling with — so the useful question is not "when should I go to Lisbon", it is "where is good in the two weeks I actually have". Every tool here works in that direction.

Real months, not yearly averages

Every guide carries month-by-month climate — the average high and low for that specific month, not a smoothed annual figure. March in Marrakesh and March in Munich are two entirely different answers, and an average hides exactly that.

When to go

Daylight decides how much fits

The most underrated input on this site. A January day in Reykjavík gives you about four usable hours; the same date in Cape Town gives fourteen. That is the difference between a five-stop day and a two-stop day, and no itinerary survives ignoring it.
