You know how many days you've got.

Length decides which cities are worth it at all. Three days suits one dense city and not a country; a week opens up loops and day trips; ten days is a different trip entirely.

Every guide here is written to a length rather than padded to fill one — a 3-day Lisbon is a genuinely different plan from a 6-day Lisbon, not the same plan stretched. Pick your number and you only see guides built for it.

The part you won't find elsewhere

Start from the thing you want to see. Not the city.

Most travel sites are organised by destination: pick a city, then read what's inside it. That's backwards for a lot of trips. Nobody decides to visit Slovenia and then discovers caves — they want to see a cave, and the real question is which one is worth the flight.

Best Of ranks one category across every destination on the site. Not a top-ten per country, but a single comparable list — every castle worth routing a day around, ranked against each other, each with the city it sits in and a link straight into that city's guide. Thirty-four categories, from the obvious to the ones nobody else bothers to rank.

On a lot of European routes, the train just wins.

Counted properly — the trip out to the airport, security, the wait, and the journey back into the city at the other end — rail beats flying on more city pairs than people expect. And on a few routes the journey is the reason to go rather than the cost of getting there.
