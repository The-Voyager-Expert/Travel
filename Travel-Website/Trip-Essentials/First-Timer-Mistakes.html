FIRST-TIMER MISTAKES

The planning mistakes first-timers make, by destination
One or two errors visitors reliably make on a first trip to each place — booking the wrong season, staying in the wrong part of town, skipping a reservation that sells out months ahead, cramming too much into a day, or underestimating how far apart things are. Not safety or scams: those live on the Safety Guide. These are the mistakes that cost you a day, not your wallet.

Nothing matches your search.

Western Europe
ParisFrance
Book aheadTurning up at the Louvre, Eiffel Tower, or Sainte-Chapelle without a timed slot. All three sell out days ahead in season and the walk-up line can run two hours. Catacombs slots go weeks out.
Over-packedStacking the Louvre and Musée d'Orsay into one day. The Louvre alone is a half-day minimum; pairing two major museums means you rush both and enjoy neither.
LondonUnited Kingdom
Wrong baseBooking near Heathrow or in outer zones to save money. The nightly saving disappears into two hours of daily Tube time — stay in Zone 1 or the near edge of Zone 2.
DistancesReading the Tube map as a real map. It is a schematic and badly distorts distance — Leicester Square to Covent Garden is one stop and a three-minute walk.
AmsterdamNetherlands
Wrong baseStaying on or beside the Red Light District for the central location. It is loud until dawn every night of the week — the Jordaan and De Pijp are ten minutes away and quiet.
Wrong seasonBooking late April for the tulips. Keukenhof peaks mid-April and the bulb fields are usually cut by the first week of May.
BarcelonaSpain
Book aheadAssuming Sagrada Família and Park Güell can be done on the day. Both are capacity-capped timed entry and routinely sell out a week or more ahead in summer.
Closing daysPlanning a big lunch at 12:30. Kitchens mostly open 1:30–4pm and dinner rarely starts before 8:30pm — arriving at Spanish opening times, not your own, changes the whole trip.
MadridSpain
Wrong seasonVisiting in August. Madrid regularly sits near 40°C and much of the city — including many of the best restaurants — closes while locals leave.
DistancesTreating Toledo, Segovia, and Ávila as one combined day trip. Each is a comfortable half-day from Madrid; all three in a day is six hours of transit.
SevilleSpain
Wrong seasonBooking July or August. Daytime temperatures pass 42°C and sightseeing outdoors becomes genuinely unpleasant — March to May and October are the windows.
Closing daysLanding during Semana Santa or Feria without knowing. Both are extraordinary to witness, but hotels triple in price and normal sightseeing largely stops.
LisbonPortugal
Over-packedUnderestimating the hills. Lisbon is built on seven of them and a day that looks flat on a map involves serious climbing — plan roughly two-thirds of what you would elsewhere.
Book aheadGoing to Sintra without a Pena Palace time slot. Entry is timed, the bus queues are long, and unticketed visitors are regularly turned away in summer.
PortoPortugal
Wrong baseBooking in Vila Nova de Gaia expecting to be "in Porto". It is across the river — fine, and the port lodges are there, but every evening ends with a bridge crossing.
DistancesAssuming the Douro Valley is a quick hop. The good stretch is two hours each way by road; it needs a full day or an overnight, not an afternoon.
DublinIreland
Over-packedGiving Dublin five days. The core sights are comfortably two — the rest of the trip belongs on the west coast or in Wicklow.
Closing daysPlanning a Sunday around shops and smaller museums. Many keep short Sunday hours or close entirely, and Good Friday still shuts a lot of the city.
EdinburghUnited Kingdom
Wrong seasonArriving in August without realising the Fringe is on. The city's population roughly doubles, accommodation prices follow, and anything unbooked is gone.
DistancesPlanning Loch Ness or Skye as a day trip. Skye is five hours each way — the Highlands need their own two or three days.
NiceFrance
Wrong baseBasing in Monaco or Cannes and commuting. Nice has the airport, the rail hub, and the cheapest beds on the coast — everything else is a short train ride from it.
Wrong seasonComing in August, when the whole of France is also on the Riviera. June and September have the same sea temperature and half the crowds.
CopenhagenDenmark
Closing daysPlanning around Tivoli in winter or early spring. It closes between seasons and only reopens for set periods — check the calendar before building a day around it.
DistancesNot realising Malmö in Sweden is 35 minutes away by train. First-timers repeatedly miss an easy second country on a short trip.
StockholmSweden
Wrong seasonBooking December for the light. Stockholm gets around six hours of daylight in midwinter, and it is dim daylight — June gives you eighteen.
Wrong baseStaying in Gamla Stan for the postcard. It is beautiful, cramped, and empties of locals after dark — Södermalm and Norrmalm are better bases.
ViennaAustria
Book aheadExpecting to see the Spanish Riding School or a Musikverein concert on a whim. Both sell out well in advance, and the standing-room tricks only work if you know the timings.
Closing daysBuilding a Monday around museums. Several of the big ones close that day, and most shops shut entirely on Sundays.
BerlinGermany
Over-packedTreating Berlin like a compact European capital. It is nine times the area of Paris — pick two districts a day, not six sights.
Wrong baseStaying around Alexanderplatz or Potsdamer Platz. Both are transit hubs with little life after work — Kreuzberg, Neukölln, and Prenzlauer Berg are where the city actually is.
PragueCzech Republic
Over-packedCrossing Charles Bridge at midday with everyone else. The same walk at 6am is empty and is the single biggest quality-of-trip difference in Prague.
Book aheadLeaving the Castle to the day itself. Timed circuits and the changing of the guard both reward a booked slot, and the queues from 10am are long.
BudapestHungary
Wrong seasonSkipping winter because of the cold. The thermal baths are at their best in the snow, and hotel prices are a fraction of summer.
Closing daysArriving at a bath at midday on a weekend. Széchenyi and Gellért are packed 11am–5pm; early morning is calm and half-empty.
Italy & the Mediterranean
RomeItaly
Book aheadNot reserving the Colosseum and Vatican Museums. Both are timed entry, both sell out, and the Vatican walk-up line can exceed three hours in summer.
Over-packedPutting the Vatican and the Forum on the same day. Each is a half-day on its feet, and doing both means seeing neither properly.
VeniceItaly
Over-packedComing for a day. Venice between 10am and 5pm is a cruise-ship crush; the city only becomes itself in the evening and early morning, so stay the night.
Wrong baseBooking on the mainland in Mestre to save money. It saves real money and costs the entire reason you came — and note the day-tripper access fee on peak dates.
FlorenceItaly
Book aheadArriving without Uffizi and Accademia reservations. Unbooked queues run two to three hours in season for both, and the David is a ten-minute visit at the end of it.
Closing daysPlanning a Monday of museums. The Uffizi, Accademia, and several others close on Mondays — make that the Tuscan hill-town day instead.
SantoriniGreece
Wrong baseBooking Fira or Perissa expecting the caldera view you saw in photos. That view is Oia and the Imerovigli ridge — everywhere else is a bus ride from it.
Wrong seasonGoing in August, when cruise passengers, heat, and prices all peak together. May, June, and late September are the same island at half the intensity.
DubrovnikCroatia
Over-packedWalking the city walls in the middle of the day. There is no shade on the circuit — do it at opening or in the last hour, not at 1pm in July.
Wrong seasonBooking peak summer, when up to four cruise ships dock at once. The Old Town in shoulder season is a genuinely different place.
AthensGreece
Book aheadClimbing to the Acropolis at midday in summer. It is timed-entry now, exposed, and frequently closed in the early afternoon during heatwaves — take the first slot.
Wrong baseBooking near Omonia because it looks central on a map. Plaka, Monastiraki, and Koukaki put you within walking distance of everything you came for.
IstanbulTürkiye
Closing daysArriving at the Hagia Sophia or Blue Mosque during prayer. Both restrict visitors around the five daily prayer times, and the Grand Bazaar closes all day Sunday.
Over-packedStaying only on the Sultanahmet side. Half the city is across the water in Beyoğlu and Kadıköy, and a trip that never crosses misses most of modern Istanbul.
Africa & the Middle East
CairoEgypt
Wrong seasonBooking June through August. Giza in summer regularly passes 40°C with no shade at all — October to April is when the pyramids are pleasant.
Over-packedSqueezing Giza, Saqqara, and the Egyptian Museum into one day. Cairo traffic alone eats three hours of that plan.
MarrakechMorocco
Wrong baseBooking a modern hotel in Gueliz for the amenities. The medina riads are the experience — and a taxi in and out of the old city every day gets old fast.
DistancesBuying a one-day Sahara tour. The real dunes at Merzouga are nine hours each way; a day trip gets you the stony Agafay desert instead.
Cape TownSouth Africa
Wrong seasonBooking the northern-hemisphere summer. July is Cape Town's cold, wet winter — December to March is the season, and it books out early.
DistancesAssuming a Kruger safari attaches easily to a Cape Town trip. It is a two-hour flight away, not a drive.
PetraJordan
Over-packedAllowing one day. The Siq to the Treasury is the first twenty minutes of a very large site — the Monastery alone is another 800 steps up.
Wrong seasonVisiting in July or August, when the canyon holds the heat. March to May and September to November are the walking seasons.
DubaiUnited Arab Emirates
Wrong seasonBooking the summer for cheap hotel rates. June to September runs above 45°C with high humidity — outdoors is effectively unusable.
Closing daysArriving during Ramadan without planning for it. Daytime eating and drinking in public is restricted and many venues shift hours entirely.
Asia
TokyoJapan
DistancesUnderestimating the scale. Tokyo is a chain of city centres — Shinjuku to Asakusa is 40 minutes, and a day of scattered pins becomes a day on trains.
Wrong baseBuying a Japan Rail Pass for a Tokyo-only trip. It does not cover the metro, and it only pays off once you are doing long Shinkansen legs.
KyotoJapan
Wrong seasonBooking cherry-blossom season without booking a hotel first. Late March to early April sells out months ahead at multiples of the normal rate.
Over-packedAttempting eight temples a day. Fushimi Inari at 7am and Arashiyama before 8am are transformative; the same places at noon are queues.
BangkokThailand
Over-packedPlanning a day around road transport. Bangkok traffic is genuinely severe — build the day along the BTS Skytrain and the river boats instead.
Closing daysArriving at the Grand Palace in shorts. The dress code is enforced, and it closes far earlier than most visitors expect at 3:30pm.
HanoiVietnam
Over-packedDoing Ha Long Bay as a day trip. It is roughly three hours each way, leaving about two hours on the water — an overnight cruise is the point of going.
Wrong baseStaying outside the Old Quarter on a short trip. Hanoi's appeal is street-level and dense; a hotel on a boulevard misses it entirely.
BaliIndonesia
DistancesBooking one hotel for the whole trip. Ubud to Uluwatu can take two hours in traffic — split the stay between the south coast and the interior.
Wrong seasonBooking January or February, the wettest months of the rainy season. April to October is dry, and the shoulder months are the best value.
SeoulSouth Korea
Closing daysTurning up at a palace on its closing day. Gyeongbokgung shuts Tuesdays and Changdeokgung Mondays — check before building the day around one.
Over-packedPlanning Seoul as a daytime city. Much of it — markets, food streets, shopping districts — runs late into the night, so front-load rest, not sights.
SingaporeSingapore
Over-packedPlanning long outdoor stretches. The humidity is constant year-round — build the day so the hottest hours land somewhere air-conditioned.
Wrong seasonWaiting for a dry season. There isn't one — it rains hard and briefly all year, so plan around afternoon showers rather than avoiding months.
The Americas
New YorkUnited States
Over-packedBooking a different neighbourhood every few hours. Twenty blocks is a mile — pick one area a day and walk it properly.
DistancesTreating the outer boroughs as far away. Brooklyn and Queens are 20 minutes on the subway and hold a great deal of what makes the city worth the trip.
San FranciscoUnited States
Wrong seasonPacking for a Californian summer. July and August are the foggiest, coldest months in the city — September and October are the warm ones.
Wrong baseBooking at Fisherman's Wharf. It is the least representative part of the city; Hayes Valley, the Mission, and North Beach all put you somewhere real.
YellowstoneUnited States
Book aheadLeaving lodging to the last minute. In-park lodges and campsites open bookings roughly a year out and fill almost immediately for summer.
DistancesUnderestimating the park. The Grand Loop is 140 miles at 45 mph with wildlife jams — two nights inside beats five days of driving in from a gateway town.
New OrleansUnited States
Wrong seasonBooking July or August. It is peak heat, humidity, and hurricane season all at once — February to May is the city at its best.
Closing daysArriving during Mardi Gras or Jazz Fest by accident. Rates multiply, the Quarter is impassable, and normal restaurant bookings vanish.
BanffCanada
Book aheadDriving to Moraine Lake. Private vehicles are no longer permitted on the access road — you need a shuttle or a licensed operator, booked in advance.
Wrong seasonBooking early June for the turquoise lakes. They are often still frozen — the colour needs late June onward.
CuscoPeru
Over-packedLanding and heading straight to Machu Picchu. Cusco sits at 3,400 m — most people need two days to acclimatise before doing anything strenuous.
Book aheadAssuming Machu Picchu tickets and Inca Trail permits are available on arrival. Permits sell out months ahead and entry is timed by circuit.
Rio de JaneiroBrazil
Wrong seasonBooking the northern summer. June to August is Rio's winter — December to March is the beach season, and Carnival prices are their own category.
Over-packedFixing Sugarloaf and Christ the Redeemer to set dates. Both are pointless in cloud — keep them flexible and go on the clear morning.
Buenos AiresArgentina
Wrong seasonBooking January. It is high summer and the city empties for the coast — October to December and March to May are far better.
DistancesAdding Iguazú as an afternoon. It is a two-hour flight north and needs two nights to be worth the trip.
Oceania
SydneyAustralia
DistancesAdding Uluru or the Great Barrier Reef as a side trip. Both are three-hour-plus flights — Australia is the size of Europe.
Wrong seasonBooking July expecting beach weather. That is winter — December to February is Sydney's summer, and school holidays land inside it.
QueenstownNew Zealand
Book aheadLeaving Milford Sound and the big walks to chance. Cruise slots and Great Walk huts both open months ahead and sell out for the season.
Wrong seasonAssuming it is a summer destination only. Queenstown is a ski town from June to September, with entirely different pricing and pace.
MauiUnited States
DistancesPlanning the Road to Hāna and Haleakalā sunrise on the same day. Hāna is a full day of driving on its own, and the summit needs a pre-dawn start.
Book aheadTurning up for Haleakalā sunrise without a reservation. Sunrise entry between 3am and 7am requires a permit booked in advance.
Iceland & the North
ReykjavíkIceland
Wrong seasonBooking June for the northern lights. There is no darkness in the Icelandic summer — the aurora season runs roughly September to March.
DistancesPlanning the Ring Road in three days. It is 1,300 km and needs a week — the Golden Circle and south coast are the short-trip versions.
