CAR RENTAL & PRIVATE TRANSFERS

Who runs which rental brand, and where each network is strong
Six brand names cover most of the world's rental fleet, and several of them are the same company selling at two price points. This page explains what each network is actually good at, and what separates a private transfer from a taxi when a car is not the answer.
Car Rental
Three groups own most of the world's rental fleet — Hertz (with Dollar and Thrifty), Avis Budget (with Budget and Payless) and Enterprise (with National and Alamo) — plus Europcar in Europe and Sixt operating independently. Because the sister brands often share one counter and one lot, the cheaper badge frequently hands over the same car with a stricter fuel policy and higher extra-driver fees. Two things decide the real cost more than the headline rate: whether the desk is inside the terminal or a shuttle ride away, and what the insurance excess and the card hold on your deposit come to.
Hertz
Global network
One of the three global networks, with the widest in-terminal airport presence — a desk you walk to rather than a shuttle to an off-site lot, which is what you want after a late arrival. Gold Plus Rewards skips the counter entirely at most large US and European airports. The group also owns Dollar and Thrifty, which sell from the same fleet at lower rates with fewer staff behind the desk.
hertz.com
Enterprise
Global network
The largest network by number of branches, and the only one with real depth away from airports — ordinary neighbourhood offices in suburbs and small towns, which is what makes a local or one-way rental practical. Service is counter-heavy by design, with a walk-round of the car at handover. Owns National, aimed at frequent business renters who pick their own car from the aisle, and Alamo, the leisure brand at lower rates.
enterprise.com
Avis
Global network
The third global name, closest in shape to Hertz: strong airport presence, business-oriented, and an Avis Preferred tier that bypasses the queue. Worth pricing against Hertz on every single booking — the two swap places constantly, and an identical car class can differ sharply depending on which of them has surplus fleet that week.
avis.com
Budget
Value brand
Avis's value brand, drawing on the same fleet and frequently the same counter for a lower headline rate and fewer inclusions. The difference shows up in the extras rather than the car: additional-driver fees, one-way charges and fuel policies are tighter, so the two need comparing on the final total rather than the day rate.
budget.com
Europcar
Europe
The largest European-headquartered network, with far deeper coverage of small cities and regional airports than the American groups manage. One thing to plan around: manual transmission is the default across most of its European fleet, so an automatic has to be booked explicitly and costs more. Owns Goldcar and InterRent, the low-cost brands common at Mediterranean holiday airports.
europcar.com
Sixt
Premium fleet
German, and independent of the three big groups. It rents a noticeably better class of car for the money — BMW, Mercedes and Audi turn up in mid-range categories where rivals offer economy hatchbacks — which makes it the one to check when the drive itself is part of the trip. Strongest in Germany and continental Europe, thinner in North America and Asia.
sixt.com
Private Transfers
A private transfer is booked and priced before the trip, so the fare cannot move with traffic, surge or the meter, and the driver waits with your name on a sign — including when the flight lands two hours late, because the booking tracks it. That is worth paying for with luggage, a group, small children, an unfamiliar language, or an arrival after midnight. For a short daytime hop in a city with functioning ride-hailing, a taxi is usually cheaper.
Blacklane
Premium
Premium professional drivers with fixed pricing worldwide.
  • Flight tracking — adjusts automatically if your flight is delayed
  • Mercedes E-Class, S-Class, SUVs, and vans
  • Meet-and-greet included
blacklane.com
Welcome Pickups
Airport Transfers
Popular for airport pickups — English-speaking drivers, competitive pricing, meet-and-greet included.
  • Strong coverage across Europe and popular tourist destinations
  • Fixed price, no surprises
welcomepickups.com
Suntransfers
Budget Transfer
Usually one of the lower-cost private transfer options — fixed pricing, no surge fees.
  • Good for resort and beach destinations
  • Book in advance for best availability
suntransfers.com
Jayride
Aggregator
Compares multiple transfer companies — choose based on reviews, vehicle type, and price.
  • Useful when you want to see all options side by side
  • Strong airport transfer coverage worldwide
jayride.com
KiwiTaxi
Budget Transfer
Fixed-price private transfers — strong coverage in Eastern Europe and Southeast Asia where other services are thin.
  • Good rates for longer airport-to-city routes
  • Book well in advance for remote destinations
kiwitaxi.com
Mozio
Aggregator
Aggregator that sometimes surfaces transfer options Jayride misses — worth checking when coverage is thin or prices look high elsewhere.
  • Covers shared shuttles, private cars, and buses
  • Good for less-common routes and smaller airports
mozio.com
Transfeero
Airport Transfers
Specializes in airport transfers — fixed price, professional drivers, strong European coverage.
  • Flight monitoring included
  • Free cancellation up to 24 hours before pickup
  • Good alternative to Welcome Pickups in Southern Europe
transfeero.com
